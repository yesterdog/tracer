# Synthetic Marathi pair for the offline end-to-end test (P3-25)

Provenance: `synthetic_hand_written`. `needs_native_review`: true. All text is invented (generic call-centre Marathi: bill query, OTP, ticket id, delivery, address, complaint, thanks). Nothing derives from any real call. This is plumbing data, not an Indic quality claim; no native speaker has seen any word.

**Verification status (2026-10-26).** The expected values were first derived by hand (the authoring session had no shell). The lead and qa then ran the REAL pipeline (`score_recorded_call` over the real Sarvam engine with an `httpx.MockTransport`, and the real `tracer score` over `--stt fake:<dir>`): the chunk plan, the base variant (H 109, S 9, D 2, I 2, MER 13/122, 10 spans, 8 flags, 9 of 120 tokens flagged, exit 0) and the non-mr variant (three results `error` `stt_unsupported_language`, exit 2) agree with every hand-derived value. The empty-chunk variant did NOT agree: the hand prediction put the three matched words of the speech-to-text tail at the start of turn 7, the aligner puts them at turns 11 and 12 (25 differences in spans E07 to E12; the totals H 73, S 4, D 43, I 1, MER 48/121, 12 flags and 46 flagged tokens were right). The pipeline is the authority: that variant's entry in `expected_results.json` was regenerated from the pipeline output and is documented, with an independent hand check, in the section "Empty-chunk variant" below. Nothing here says anything about Marathi quality or about Sarvam.

## WAV recipe (generated by the test, never committed)

Mono, 24000 Hz, 16-bit little-endian, 1,632,000 frames (68.00 s). Sample `n` of a tone segment is `int(round(8000 * sin(2*pi*f*n/24000)))` with `n` the index in the whole file; a silence segment is exact zeros. Segments (seconds): tone 440 Hz 0-9.80; silence 9.80-10.20; tone 523 Hz 10.20-22.00; **silence 22.00-23.00**; tone 440 Hz 23.00-35.80; silence 35.80-36.20; tone 523 Hz 36.20-45.00; **silence 45.00-46.00**; tone 440 Hz 46.00-57.80; silence 57.80-58.20; tone 523 Hz 58.20-68.00. Exact frames are in `wav_recipe.json`.

To write the recording for a manual run: `python make_wav.py synthetic_mr.wav` (standard library only; it reads `wav_recipe.json` beside it).

With the defaults (window 600000, search region the last 120000, quiet frame 480) the only all-zero 20 ms frames in each search region lie inside the bold silences; a tie goes to the latest. Cut 1 = 551760 (22.99 s). Chunk 2 starts there, so its frame grid is shifted by 240 and cut 2 = 1103520. Chunks: 0 to 551760, 551760 to 1103520, 1103520 to 1632000 (528480 frames, 22.02 s). The three other silences are outside both regions on purpose. Only the zeros matter; the tone values do not enter any expected result.

## Empty-chunk variant (`stt_empty_chunk/`): the pipeline is the authority, with an independent check

Chunk 1 answers `{"transcript": "", "language_code": null}`: no speech-to-text turn is made for it (ADR 0004 Decision 10b), the variant has two speech-to-text turns and 78 tokens, and the middle of the call exists only in the export. The comparison keys are the same as in the base variant.

**What the aligner does (ADR 0023 rules 6 to 8, `align.py`).** The cost of an alignment is the pair (edits, deletions+insertions), compared lexicographically: match (0, 0), substitution (1, 0), deletion (1, 1), insertion (1, 1). Among alignments of minimal cost the backtrace from the end prefers, at each step, match > substitution > deletion > insertion.

**The stretch.** After the matched words `मिनिट थांबा` (export tokens 40 and 41, speech-to-text tokens 39 and 40) the speech-to-text side has four tokens left before the matched `हो` of turn 15: the tail of chunk 0, `thik aahe mi thaamb` (keys of the words `ठीक`, `आहे`, `मी`, `थांब`; speech-to-text tokens 41 to 44). The export side has 46 tokens (export tokens 42 to 87, turns 7 to 14).

**Hand derivation.**
1. The word `thaamb` occurs nowhere in the export (turn 7 has `thaambto`, turn 6 has `thaambaa`, both different words), so at most three of the four speech-to-text tokens can match.
2. Every export token that is not paired is a deletion (one edit, one indel). Three matches and one substitution leave 46 - 4 = 42 deletions: cost (1 + 42, 42) = (43, 42). Two matches and two substitutions cost (44, 42); a speech-to-text token left unpaired is an insertion and costs one more edit and one more indel. So the minimum is (43, 42), and it is reached exactly when `thik`, `aahe`, `mi` match in order and `thaamb` is substituted for an export token after the `mi` match.
3. That is a tie among many placements. `thik` occurs in the stretch at export 42 and 63, `aahe` at 43, 52, 64, 72, 75, 79 and 86, `mi` at 44 and 68. The hand prediction took the first occurrences (42, 43, 44) and a substitution on the last token (87); that alignment also costs (43, 42), but the rule above chooses differently.
4. The backtrace starts at the end. At the cell of export token 87 with `thaamb` still to place, a substitution ties with a deletion and wins, so `thaamb` is substituted for export 87 (`का`, the last word of turn 14, at the chunk edge). Going back with `mi` to place, the stored step is a deletion for export 86 down to 69 (a substitution there would cost one edit more than the best remaining alignment, which still has a match to give) and a match at export 68, the LATEST `mi` before the substitution. The same reasoning gives the latest `aahe` before 68, which is export 64, and the latest `thik` before 64, which is export 63. Every earlier export token of the stretch is a deletion.
5. Span boundaries follow from the export text (offsets are code points of the turn): turn 7 `thik aahe, mi thaambto` is wholly deleted, offsets 0 to 18 (4 tokens); turn 8 offsets 0 to 47 (7 tokens); turn 9 0 to 24 (5); turn 10 0 to 35 (5); turn 11 `thik aahe, kripaya otp paathava` keeps `thik aahe` (matches 63 and 64) and deletes `kripaya otp paathava`, offsets 9 to 24 (3 tokens); turn 12 `mi mobaailvar otp paathvla aahe` keeps `mi` (match 68) and deletes the other four tokens, offsets 3 to 26; turn 13 0 to 30 (7 tokens); turn 14 offsets 0 to 37 (8 tokens: 7 deletions and the substitution of `thaamb`, speech-to-text token range 44 to 45). Maximal runs of non-match ops are cut at turn boundaries, so turns 7 to 10 are four spans (speech-to-text gap at token 41), turn 11 one span (gap at 43), turns 12 to 14 three spans (gap at 44).
6. Counts: 42 deletions in the stretch plus `एक` of turn 6 gives D 43; substitutions `500`, `khup`, `thaamb`, `vel` give S 4; the matches are 120 - 43 - 4 = 73; one insertion (`nakki`) gives I 1; edits 48, MER 48 / (73 + 48) = 48/121. Flagged tokens: every span of the stretch has confidence at least 0.9429 (flagged), so 4 + 7 + 5 + 5 + 3 + 4 + 7 + 8 = 43 plus `D04`, `D05`, `D06` give 46.

**Independent check.** `check_empty_chunk_alignment.py` (next to the red logs of batch F) and the test `test_synthetic_expectations_match_an_independent_alignment` run a second implementation of the rule above (written without `align.py` and `spans.py`; only the public tokeniser supplies the comparison keys) and reproduce the counts and every span with export tokens (turn, offsets, token and substitution and deletion counts) of both the base and the empty-chunk variants. The test `test_synthetic_empty_chunk_tie_is_real_and_broken_by_the_latest_feasible_word` builds the hand-predicted alignment explicitly and shows that it costs (48, 44) like the chosen one, so the placement is a tie broken by the rule, not a disagreement about the minimum.

Not independent: the tokeniser and normaliser (`mr-Deva/1`) are the real ones, and the Marathi words have no native review (BL-169).
